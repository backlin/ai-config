# The Power of Connected Expertise
March 17, 2026 - Interview

Johan Rudberg, CEO at Influence, a Newground company, discusses why shared expertise rather than ownership drives growth and opportunity.

Rudberg uses the daycare late-pickup example to illustrate incentive structures. When a fee was introduced for parents arriving late, more showed up tardy because the financial cost replaced the social embarrassment. He notes: "The problem is that if you try to roll it back, people remember it."

He emphasizes the dangers of explicit incentive models in complex professional services, explaining that clients become uncomfortable when they suspect kickback arrangements influence referrals.

Rudberg clarifies the fundamental distinction: "Our profession is not about opening the door for our colleagues. It's about opening the door for our clients, to give them access to the capability, competence, and knowledge within one of our alliance companies. And that's something you can't put a price on."

The core message centers on trust, transparency, and client-first values within the alliance structure rather than transaction-based relationships.
