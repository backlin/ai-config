# Spotlight article

Spotlight articles appear on the Newground alliance website. They profile companies, people, and engagements within the alliance. The audience is potential clients, partners, and recruits.

## Sanity check before writing content
When user invokes this skill to write a piece, ensure that all the necessary information has been provided (as expained in the Output section),
or can be created with little ambiguity.

## Output
### Document format

```
# Article Title
Month DD, YYYY - Type

Body paragraphs...
```

- Line 1: H1 title
- Line 2: date and type as plain text, format `March 19, 2026 - Case`
- No other markdown formatting in the body (no bold, italic, links, subheadings, bullet lists)

### Article types

**Interview** — Profiles a person or founding team and their company within Newground. Narrative paragraphs interwoven with direct quotes. Opens by introducing who they are and what they do; closes with a forward-looking or thematic statement. Typically 4-5 paragraphs.

**Case** — Tells the story of a client engagement. Chronological arc: starting situation → how the scope evolved → how it was delivered → outcomes. Quotes from a key participant. Mention concrete results (timeline, budget, adoption). Close with a broader takeaway about the collaboration model. Longest format, ~10 paragraphs.

**News** — Covers an initiative, event, or industry development connected to an alliance company. Opens with industry context, then alternates narrative paragraphs and standalone quotes. Quotes may not be individually attributed by name in every instance. ~8-10 paragraphs.

**Perspectives** — Shortest format. A brief intro sets context, then an extended direct quote (multiple paragraphs) from one named person conveys a viewpoint. ~3 paragraphs total.

### Voice and tone

- Journalistic third person. Professional, understated, never promotional or salesy.
- Declarative sentences, medium length. No exclamation marks.
- Direct quotes use double quotation marks woven into prose — never blockquotes.
- Attribution verbs: says, explains, notes, clarifies.
- First mention of a person uses full name + role/title; subsequent mentions use last name or first name only.
- Avoid jargon, buzzwords, and marketing language.

### Recurring themes

Every article connects its subject back to at least one of these:

- Specialist expertise over generalist scale
- Collaboration across firms within the alliance
- Trust, culture, and psychological safety
- Long-term value over short-term deliverables
- The Newground alliance as enabler/backdrop (never the primary subject)

### What to avoid

- Calls to action or sales pitches
- Superlatives and hype ("world-class", "cutting-edge", "game-changing")
- Abstract claims without grounding in specifics
- Making Newground itself the hero — the featured company or person is the subject
